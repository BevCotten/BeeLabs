# Episode 001 — Limi Voice Recording Sheet

## Series

*Adventures with Limi*

## Performance Guide

- Use a bright, curious toddler voice without forcing a squeaky pitch.
- Keep words clear and emotions easy for children ages 2–4 to recognize.
- Record one scene at a time.
- Leave about two seconds between lines.
- Keep AutoPitch off for spoken dialogue.
- Save every scene as a separate take so individual lines can be adjusted later.

## Scene 1 — Choosing Sneakers

**Direction:** Playful, confident, and excited. Limi is talking to himself and inviting the audience into his fun.

1. “Purple shoes?”
2. “Ooooh!”
3. “Or green shoes?”
4. “Purple…green…purple…green!”
5. “Which pair would you choose?”
6. “Hmm…I choose—”

## Scene 2 — The Haircut Announcement

**Direction:** Begin cheerful, then become surprised and quietly nervous. “I’m not nervous” should sound like Limi is trying to convince himself.

1. “Good morning, Mommy!”
2. “My…haircut?”
3. “I’m not nervous.”

## Scene 3 — Jazz Notices

**Direction:** Honest, thoughtful, and slightly uncertain; finish with a burst of determination.

1. “My tummy feels wiggly.”
2. “But I’m brave!”
3. “Nervous…and brave?”
4. “Tiggy needs a fresh cut!”

## Scene 4 — Practicing With Tiggy

**Direction:** Energetic pretend play. Each step should sound rhythmic and easy for toddlers to repeat.

1. “Sit in the chair!”
2. “Cape on!”
3. “Buzzzzzzz!”
4. “Looking fresh, Tiggy!”
5. “Can Tiggy come too?”

## Scene 5 — Team Limi at Breakfast

**Direction:** Start worried, then become reassured and proud.

1. “The clippers might sound too loud.”
2. “I can say, ‘Break, please!’”
3. “Let’s light it up!”

## Scene 6 — Arriving at the Barbershop

**Direction:** Quietly observant at first; confidence grows with each line.

1. “A big chair.”
2. “And a mirror!”
3. “I’m ready to go inside.”

## Scene 7 — Meeting Ms. Toni

**Direction:** Shy but friendly. The fist-bump line should sound like Limi has made a proud choice.

1. “This is Tiggy.”
2. “Which greeting should I choose?”
3. “Fist bump!”

## Scene 8 — Hearing the Clippers

**Direction:** Use clear emotional movement: prepared, startled, then cautiously brave.

1. “First, the chair. Next, the cape.”
2. “That’s loud!”
3. “A little closer.”

## Scene 9 — Break, Please

**Direction:** Uncomfortable but understandable; “Break, please” should be calm, clear, and strong.

1. “It feels itchy.”
2. “Break, please!”
3. “I need to move!”

## Scene 10 — Wiggle Break

**Direction:** Playful, physical, and increasingly regulated.

1. “Wiggle, wiggle, wiggle!”
2. “Reach up high!”
3. “My body feels ready.”
4. “Yes, please!”

## Scene 11 — Still-as-a-Statue Game

**Direction:** Focused and playful, followed by genuine surprise.

1. “I am a statue.”
2. “Already?”

## Scene 12 — Mirror Reveal

**Direction:** Build from eager anticipation to delighted surprise and genuine pride.

1. “I’m ready!”
2. “Whoa!”
3. “That’s me?”
4. “I was nervous…and I did it!”

## Scene 13 — Light It Up

**Direction:** Celebratory, rhythmic, and directed toward the audience. Leave room for audience responses where indicated.

1. “First, I sat in the chair!”
2. “Next, I wore the cape!”
3. “Then, I heard the clippers!”
4. “Buzzzzz!”
5. “Last, I looked in the mirror!”
6. “I said, ‘Break, please!’”
7. “Did you try the breathing and wiggle break with me?”
8. **Pause for audience response.**
9. “You did? We were brave together!”
10. “When something feels new, I can breathe.”
11. “We learned something new—”
