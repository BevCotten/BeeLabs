# Episode 002 — Limi Recording Guide

**Episode:** *Limi’s First Dental Visit*  
**Minimum:** Record **at least two takes of every dialogue line**.

## Performance Guide
- Curious, expressive toddler energy; natural rather than exaggerated.
- Keep words clear for ages 2–4.
- Let his confidence grow as the episode progresses.
- Leave about two seconds between lines.
- Preserve written pauses and leave real response space when speaking to the audience.

## Scene 1 — Morning Brush
**Direction:** Playful and silly. Limi is enjoying the lion game while brushing.
1. “Aaaaah!”
2. “ROAR!”
3. “Roar!”

## Scene 2 — Dentist Day
**Direction:** Start surprised and uncertain, not frightened. The audience question should sound genuinely curious.
1. “The dentist?”
2. “Have you been to the dentist before?”
3. “I have questions.”

## Scene 3 — Limi Has Questions
**Direction:** Genuine curiosity. He wants to understand what will happen.
1. “What happens at the dentist?”
2. “What if I don’t know what something does?”

## Scene 4 — The New-Thing Plan
**Direction:** Learn the steps carefully, then become confident and excited while inviting the audience to join.
1. “Ask...look...try.”
2. “...then I can try!”
3. “Can you do it with me?”
4. “Ask!”
5. “Look!”
6. “Try!”
7. “Show me first, then I can try!”
8. “Come on, Tiggy. We have a plan.”

## Scene 7 — Fist Bump
**Direction:** Friendly and increasingly comfortable. He politely asks for the greeting he wants.
1. “It’s nice to meet you, Dr. Miles. Can we do a fist bump?”

## Scene 8 — Choosing the Chair
**Direction:** Confident choice, followed by imaginative excitement. Build the countdown naturally into “blast off!”
1. “The chair.”
2. “I am on a rocket ship! One, two, three... blast off!”

## Scene 9 — The Bright Light
**Direction:** Move from polite uncertainty to delighted recognition, then calm confidence.
1. “Yes, please.”
2. “Ohhh! It’s like a flashlight!”
3. “I’m ready.”

## Scene 10 — The Tiny Mirror
**Direction:** Curious and observant. The second line should sound like he is remembering his plan and making his own decision.
1. “Tiny mirror.”
2. “I looked...and now I can try.”

## Scene 12 — Show Me Again, Please
**Direction:** Surprised but not panicked. “Show me again, please” is calm, clear self-advocacy. The final three lines become increasingly confident.
1. “Show me again, please.”
2. “I asked.”
3. “I looked.”
4. “Now I can try.”

## Scene 13 — Sparkly Smile, Light It Up
**Direction:** Proud, joyful payoff. Keep the lesson rhythmic and leave genuine space for audience response.
1. “Ooooh!”
2. “My teeth are shiny!”
3. “I asked...”
4. “I looked...”
5. “And then I tried!”
6. “Did you practice with me?”
7. “We did it!”
8. “When something is new—”
9. “Show me first, then I can try!”
10. “We learned something new—”
