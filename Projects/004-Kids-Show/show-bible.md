# Adventures with Limi — Show Bible

## Target Audience

Children ages **2–4**.

The show will use:

- Simple language
- Repetition
- Music and movement
- Bright, friendly visuals
- Short stories
- Gentle educational and emotional lessons

## Show Purpose

The show combines early learning with emotional and everyday life skills.

Each episode will help children practice concepts such as:

- Colors, numbers, letters, and shapes
- Naming and managing feelings
- Kindness, sharing, and patience
- Problem-solving and confidence
- Healthy routines and independence

The lessons will be taught through playful stories, music, repetition, and movement.

## Project Origin

*Adventures with Limi* began with an original children’s-show idea from **James**. Bee developed the characters, world, educational purpose, visual identity, and stories from that foundation.

The project is inspired by their family and their shared love for their children.

## Main Character

The main character is a curious human toddler who learns by exploring, asking questions, making small mistakes, and trying again.

The toddler speaks directly to the audience and invites children to answer questions, sing, move, count, and solve simple problems during each adventure.

## Character Inspiration

The main character is inspired by Sani’s curiosity, personality, energy, and experience of discovering the world as a toddler.

The character will feel authentic and relatable while remaining a fictionalized version created specifically for the show.

## Character Name

The main character’s name is **Limi** (pronounced **LEE-mee**).

Limi’s name is inspired by the Haitian Creole word *limyè*, meaning **light**. He is a curious little light who discovers something new everywhere he goes.

### Limi’s Core Personality

- Curious
- Cheerful
- Affectionate
- Energetic
- Sometimes impatient
- Willing to try again
- Excited to include the audience

## Limi’s Appearance

Limi is a Black toddler with:

- Soft, super-curly **4B hair**
- Bright, expressive eyes
- A warm smile
- Highly animated facial expressions
- A face that clearly shows curiosity, excitement, confusion, frustration, and pride

His expressions help young viewers recognize and name emotions throughout each episode.
 
## Limi’s Signature Style

Limi has a fresh, sporty toddler style.

His signature outfit includes:

- A clean graphic T-shirt
- Basketball shorts
- Fresh athletic sneakers
- Coordinating socks
- A small diamond stud in one ear
- No visible brand logos

His outfit is comfortable enough for dancing, running, exploring, and joining every adventure.
## Signature Color Palette

Limi’s signature colors are **deep purple, electric lime, and crisp white**.

His primary outfit includes a purple T-shirt, basketball shorts with lime details, fresh white athletic sneakers, coordinating socks, and his signature diamond stud.

A small light or spark symbol connects his clothing to the meaning behind his name.
## Limi’s World

Limi lives in a warm, recognizable neighborhood filled with everyday places such as his home, the park, grocery store, library, doctor’s office, barbershop, and childcare center.

Each real-life situation sparks an imaginative learning adventure. Limi’s imagination makes the lesson playful, but the episode always returns to the real world so children can understand how the lesson applies to their own lives.

The show helps toddlers prepare for experiences such as:

- Meeting new people
- Visiting the doctor or dentist
- Going to childcare
- Sharing and taking turns
- Cleaning up
- Trying unfamiliar foods
- Handling big feelings
- Following safety rules
- Separating from a parent temporarily
- Making mistakes and trying again
## Limi’s Family

Limi lives with two loving parents and his big sister.

His family helps him make sense of the real world without immediately solving every problem for him. They listen, explain, model healthy communication, and encourage Limi to try things himself.

### Family Roles

- **Parent One:** Warm, playful, and emotionally reassuring
- **Parent Two:** Practical, funny, and encouraging
- **Big Sister:** Protective, honest, and patient; she helps Limi understand things from a child-friendly perspective

The family experiences ordinary moments together, including meals, errands, celebrations, disagreements, routines, and new experiences.

## Tiggy

Tiggy is Limi’s beloved stuffed tiger and comfort companion. Limi brings Tiggy along when an experience feels new, uncertain, or intimidating.

Tiggy does not speak independently. Instead, Limi and his family use pretend play with Tiggy to rehearse unfamiliar routines, explain new experiences, and help Limi express feelings that may be difficult to name. Tiggy supports the learning without replacing Limi’s own courage, choices, or voice.

### Tiggy’s Story Role

- Helps Limi practice unfamiliar experiences through pretend play
- Provides familiar comfort without being treated as a magical solution
- Encourages sequencing, emotional expression, and imaginative learning
- Remains small enough for Limi to carry naturally
- Appears when the story benefits from comfort or rehearsal rather than in every scene
### Big Sister

Limi’s big sister is a teenager. Despite their age difference, they share a close, playful bond.

She:

- Helps Limi name and understand his feelings
- Explains confusing situations in simple language
- Plays music, dances, and acts silly with him
- Sometimes takes him on everyday outings
- Encourages him to try things independently
- Occasionally becomes impatient or frustrated and models how to apologize
- Has her own age-appropriate challenges, showing that learning and growing never stop
- Learns from Limi just as he learns from her
## Cultural Representation

Limi and his family are a modern Black family. Their identity is represented naturally through their appearance, hair, clothing, family relationships, music, humor, and everyday experiences.

The show is designed to feel welcoming and relatable to children from many different backgrounds. Limi’s name retains its special connection to the Haitian Creole word *limyè*, meaning light, without making Haitian culture the central subject of the series.
#### Jasmine “Jazz”

Limi’s teenage sister is named **Jasmine**, but everyone calls her **Jazz**.

Jazz is **16 years old**.

Jazz is confident, funny, expressive, and protective of Limi. She loves music, style, and spending time with her friends, but she always makes room for her little brother.

Limi looks up to Jazz and often copies her. Their relationship creates opportunities to teach boundaries, patience, apologies, cooperation, and the special bond between siblings.

### Jazz’s Personality and Role

Jazz is confident, outspoken, caring, protective, and stylish.

She does not simply give Limi answers. She asks questions, helps him think, and consistently steers him toward safe, kind, and responsible choices. When Limi makes a mistake, Jazz corrects him without shaming him.

Jazz is also willing to admit when she is wrong, making her a realistic role model rather than a perfect character.

## Mandatory Character Prompt Standard

Every image-generation and animation prompt must include a complete locked description for every visible character. Do not rely only on phrases such as “keep the character the same,” “use the previous design,” or “as before.” Reference images must still be supplied whenever the tool supports them.

### Required Character Details

Describe each visible character in this order:

1. **Identity and role:** Name, age or age range, cultural identity when relevant, and relationship to Limi.
2. **Height and proportions:** Exact or relative height, build, age-appropriate body proportions, and size relative to the other visible characters.
3. **Complexion:** Locked skin tone and undertone.
4. **Facial structure:** Face shape, forehead, cheek shape, jawline, chin, eyebrows, eye shape and color, nose shape, lip shape, smile, facial hair, and any other defining feature.
5. **Hair:** Color, texture, curl or loc pattern, length, density, part, hairline, style, and any gray or accent color.
6. **Clothing:** Every garment, its exact color, fit, fabric or texture when visually important, trim, graphics, sleeve length, waist details, and layering.
7. **Footwear:** Exact shoe type, colors, laces, soles, socks, and the number and placement of shoes.
8. **Accessories:** Earrings, necklaces, bracelets, watches, rings, wedding bands, hair accessories, glasses, and their exact placement.
9. **Personality cues:** Normal posture, energy, signature expressions, and the emotional quality the character should communicate.
10. **Scene-specific state:** Pose, action, expression, direction of gaze, objects held, and whether the character is speaking or listening.
11. **Locked exclusions:** State exactly what must not be added, removed, recolored, duplicated, resized, aged, or redesigned.

### Prompt Construction Order

Each prompt should follow this order:

1. Locked character descriptions
2. Approved location and background description
3. Scene action and emotional beat
4. Speaker and lip-sync instructions
5. Camera, framing, and lighting
6. Object and continuity requirements
7. Explicit negative restrictions

### Consistency Rules

- Repeat the important locked traits in every prompt, even when a reference image is attached.
- Name exact colors instead of using vague descriptions such as “bright,” “dark,” or “colorful.”
- State relative heights whenever two or more characters share a frame.
- State who is speaking and require every other visible person to keep their mouth closed.
- Specify the exact number and placement of shoes, earrings, rings, tools, toys, and other easily duplicated objects.
- Keep established outfits unchanged unless a story-approved wardrobe change is documented first.
- Use the approved character reference image whenever available.
- Generate fewer visible characters when accurate identity or lip-sync is more important than a group composition.
- Inspect every result before animation. Reject facial drift, altered hair, incorrect complexion, changed body proportions, wardrobe changes, missing jewelry, duplicate objects, or extra people.

Detailed prompts and reference images reduce character drift but cannot guarantee that a generative model will never make an error. Every generated panel must still pass the continuity review before it is approved.
